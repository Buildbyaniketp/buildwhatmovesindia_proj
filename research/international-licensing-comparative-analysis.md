# International learner-driver service comparison

**Date:** 24 August 2026

**Method:** Structured desk/heuristic review of first-party government services

**Jurisdictions:** United Kingdom, New Zealand, New South Wales (Australia), Singapore, and Ontario (Canada)
**Decision:** Which interaction patterns should inform the Licence Path Journey Passport POC?

## Executive answer

The comparison does not identify one superior end-to-end model to copy. Instead, official services expose several useful components: licensing progressions, up-front prerequisites, named recovery routes, task-specific support, and clear owners for selected actions. Combining those components into one persistent guidance layer is a **Licence Path design hypothesis**, not an observed international standard or demonstrated outcome. Account continuity is uneven: New South Wales explicitly autosaves progress in its online learning product, the UK exposes account-based post-submission tracking but public mid-application saving is unverified, and no comparable draft-resume model was observed on the cited unauthenticated New Zealand, Singapore or Ontario pages.

For Licence Path, the internationally observed patterns worth testing are therefore:

1. a stable eight-stage Journey Passport;
2. conditional readiness before each official action;
3. a fictional profile with precise local-device save boundaries;
4. recovery beside the blocked stage;
5. task-level language/accessibility support; and
6. an unmistakable hand-off to Sarathi.

These are official-service observations and design hypotheses, not participant findings or proof that they will improve Indian task success.

## Evidence-led patterns to use

### P1 — Published progressions suggest testing a persistent next-action map

Several audited services publish a licensing progression or prerequisite sequence outside individual transactions [UK-05, NZ-01, AU-04, CA-01, SG-04]. This suggests testing an Indian stage map that remains visible while the prototype focuses one task at a time.

**Contradicting/complicating evidence:** The stage counts and operating models differ substantially; none supports copying an eight-stage Indian sequence verbatim.

**POC design hypothesis:** Retain the official West Bengal stages inside the Journey Passport. Each stage card shows status, prerequisite, next action and official/prototype ownership. Whether this improves comprehension remains to be tested.

### P2 — Front-load eligibility and readiness

Every audited jurisdiction publishes at least some eligibility, identity, document, test or channel prerequisites before or alongside the start/apply entry point [UK-01, UK-03, NZ-02, NZ-03, AU-01, AU-05, SG-01, SG-05, CA-01, CA-02]. New Zealand's interactive ID checker is especially relevant because it guides a document choice without claiming to verify identity [NZ-03].

**Contradicting/complicating evidence:** Some guidance is still spread across pages or channels. Publication does not prove citizens find or understand it.

**POC design hypothesis:** Add a “Before you continue” summary to every deep stage: what applies to Maya, what is missing, what happens on Sarathi, and how to resolve it.

### P3 — Save progress only with explicit boundaries

NSW explicitly states that its DKT Online course autosaves when the browser closes, while the current quiz may be lost and the final test must occur in one session [AU-03]. The UK provides account-based tracking after submission [UK-07], but current public evidence does not confirm mid-application autosave [UK-06]. No comparable draft-resume model was observed on the cited unauthenticated New Zealand, Singapore or Ontario pages [NZ-04, SG-03, CA-03].

Indian evidence makes the need more direct: Parivahan says an expired LL/DL application session saves no data and the applicant must apply again; later modules use application number/date of birth/CAPTCHA rather than a unified citizen profile.

**Contradicting/complicating evidence:** A government account introduces privacy, security, recovery and cross-device expectations that a browser-only POC cannot satisfy.

**POC design hypothesis:** Create “My Licence Path” as an explicitly fictional device-only profile. Show last saved stage, next action, “Saved on this device,” close/reopen continuity, and “Clear demo progress.” Never render a password, OTP, identity-verification or cloud-sync claim.

### P4 — Published recovery routes suggest testing contextual recovery

The audited services publish recovery routes for selected failures: booking-access help in the UK [UK-09], switching channels, retakes, refunds and troubleshooting in NSW [AU-10], failure and support routes in New Zealand [NZ-08], and alternative channels for selected Singapore issues [SG-10]. The sources do not establish that recovery appears beside the exact failure point or improves outcomes.

**Contradicting/complicating evidence:** Published recovery routes do not show whether they work well in practice.

**POC design hypothesis:** Test context-linked recovery beside each blocked Passport stage. Slot recovery changes the illustrative date; document recovery returns to the missing proof; upstream changes explain which prepared states were reset.

### P5 — Official services expose task-specific language and accessibility options

The cited official pages document UK theory-test accommodations; New Zealand test languages plus reader, interpreter and oral options; six-language NSW online testing with other-language/interpreter support routed in person; Singapore theory-learning material in four languages; and Ontario G1 knowledge-test translation into Ojibway, Oji-Cree and Cree [UK-10, NZ-07, AU-09, SG-09, CA-04].

**Contradicting/complicating evidence:** Multilingual guidance does not guarantee multilingual transactions, and a country-specific language policy cannot be transferred to India.

**POC design hypothesis:** Keep English/Hindi parity across the same journey; expose assistance beside categories, documents and slot booking. Treat Bangla and other regional languages as a language-ready architecture requirement, not delivered coverage. The sources do not establish comprehension, completion or accessibility effectiveness.

### P6 — Official journeys assign specific actions to named owners

Across the audited services, official guidance distinguishes at least some transaction, testing, account, agency or physical-service responsibilities [UK-12, NZ-10, AU-02, AU-07, SG-11, CA-03]. The evidence does not show that every jurisdiction separates every owner type or that citizens understand the boundary.

**Contradicting/complicating evidence:** Official services can rely on government branding and verified records; Licence Path cannot.

**POC safety requirement to test:** Keep the independent-prototype banner persistent. Label stages “Prepared in prototype” or “Official action required.” Name Sarathi/RTO as the next owner and never generate official-looking receipts, credentials, payment references or success states.

## Patterns not to copy

- Government identity, biometrics, verified prefill or credentials from GOV.UK accounts, MyServiceNSW or Singpass.
- Foreign ages, licence classes, documents, fees, retake rules, timelines, languages or appointment policies.
- Official-looking application status, payments, receipts, bookings or digital licences.
- NSW's data-heavy multi-hour online learning course for this mobile-first POC [AU-08].
- Singapore's driving-centre pass-rate comparison without an equivalent Indian policy/use case [SG-07].
- New Zealand's external-agent operating model or Ontario's walk-in model.
- UK mid-application autosave as a claimed current feature; it was not publicly verified [UK-06].
- Official government visual trust cues that could weaken the independent-concept disclosure.

## Human review flags

- Decide whether a future production concept should propose a government-authenticated citizen profile, or a lower-risk encrypted draft token. The POC cannot settle that architecture.
- Decide whether “My Licence Path” should cover only LL applications or become a cross-service transport profile; expanding it changes privacy and governance scope substantially.
- Confirm whether testing in Hindi without Bangla participation is acceptable for a West Bengal illustration; do not generalize language findings.

## Limitations

- This is a purposive sample of five jurisdictions, selected for service-design contrast, not a representative global benchmark or league table.
- Public unauthenticated pages cannot reveal all behind-login account, draft-save or transaction behaviour.
- Laws, delivery channels and identity infrastructure differ; only interaction patterns are compared.
- Mobile observations were narrow page checks, not accessibility conformance audits.
- No citizens used these services in this study. Patterns remain desk observations and recommendations remain hypotheses until tested.

## Open questions

1. Do first-time applicants understand the Journey Passport better than the current shortened POC?
2. Does a fictional device-only profile create false expectations of a real government account?
3. Which saved-state details are useful without encouraging sensitive-data entry?
4. Can users correctly teach back what is prepared versus what remains official?
