# International comparison — independent validation record

**Date:** 24 August 2026

**Object reviewed:** `research/international-licensing-comparative-analysis.md`
**Evidence standard:** Current official government pages only; desk evidence, not participant findings

## Panel method

Three validators independently reviewed claims P1–P6 against the same evidence-ID corpus without seeing one another's judgments. Each returned `Supported`, `Partially supported`, or `Unsupported`, cited evidence IDs, and required wording corrections. P5 and P6 did not receive a unanimous verdict, so two additional cold validators reviewed those claims. The final report uses the more conservative interpretation where the panel split.

## Agreement and decision

| Claim | Initial panel | Expanded panel | Final treatment |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| P1 — journey progression | 3/3 Partially supported | — | Retained as an observed progression plus a POC hypothesis; no effectiveness claim |
| P2 — front-loaded prerequisites | 3/3 Supported | — | Retained; changed “before commitment” to “before or alongside start/apply” |
| P3 — explicit save boundaries | 3/3 Partially supported | — | Retained with limits; corrected NSW citation and narrowed absence language |
| P4 — recovery routes | 3/3 Partially supported | — | Retained as published routes; colocated recovery is explicitly a hypothesis |
| P5 — task-linked language/access support | 1 Supported, 2 Partially supported | 1 Supported, 4 Partially supported (80% exact agreement) | Conservatively Partially supported; corrected Ontario description and removed outcome inference |
| P6 — named action owners | 2 Supported, 1 Partially supported | 2 Supported, 3 Partially supported (60% exact agreement) | Conservatively Partially supported; narrowed to “at least some” owner/channel distinctions |

No validator rated a claim Unsupported. The main disagreement was not whether the cited interface elements exist, but whether the original synthesis implied a repeated integrated architecture or demonstrated user benefit. The final report separates three levels throughout: **official observation**, **POC design hypothesis/safety requirement**, and **demonstrated outcome (none)**.

## Corrections required by the panel

1. Replace the “strongest recurring guidance layer” statement with discrete observed components; the integrated Journey Passport is a product hypothesis.
2. Do not imply persistent whole-journey visibility or one-next-action focus is a proven international pattern.
3. Use the current Service NSW DKT common-questions page for the explicit autosave boundaries.
4. Say no comparable draft-resume model was **observed on cited unauthenticated pages**, not that none exists.
5. Describe published recovery routes separately from the proposed placement beside a blocked stage.
6. Describe Ontario evidence as G1 test translation into Ojibway, Oji-Cree and Cree; do not claim a broader delivery-channel change.
7. Describe official journeys as distinguishing at least some responsible organisations/channels; do not imply all five jurisdictions separate every owner type.
8. Treat the P0/P1/P2 rankings as five-day product decisions, not research findings.

## Transferability consensus

- **Transferable:** a conspicuous independent-prototype → Sarathi/RTO boundary.
- **Transferable with caveat:** stable Journey Passport; fictional device-only continuity; conditional readiness; contextual recovery; slot guidance; bilingual task-level help; non-numeric cost/duration ownership.
- **Not transferable:** foreign policy, ages, licence classes, documents, fees, waiting periods, identity systems, official trust cues, or government transaction/status artefacts.

## Residual uncertainty

Public pages do not reveal every signed-in or behind-transaction behaviour. None of this evidence establishes findability, comprehension, task success, accessibility conformance, or reduced abandonment. Those remain questions for the short Licence Path usability test.
